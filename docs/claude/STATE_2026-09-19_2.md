### Audit items for the paper: what a reviewer can attack, claim by claim (Claude, 2026-09-19; A1–A4 were shown to Kian in chat, he asked for the list to be recorded; A5–A8 and the second remedy of A2 were added while writing this file)
- A1, "unchanged under a second labeling". The registered noise subset relabeled C-B09 and E-B06. At both cuts the first new node is Planning in 25 of 25 continuations under both labelings; at E-B06 every continuation opens with a "Wait" sentence, which rule R4 labels in code whatever the judge says; and C-B09's last label, Reflection, is a singleton in the C-trace and is excluded from the C-trace test (it enters only the pooled test). The check had almost no way to fail, and it says nothing about the cuts that drive S, the Reasoning group C-B03, B04, B05, B06, B10, B11. Options, Kian decides: (i) reword — report the noise floor (Level 1 kappa 0.78 and 0.83 on this run's continuations) and drop the robustness claim; (ii) a judge-free version of the type-only test on the text of the first new sentence (case opener "If …", numbered heading, "Wait", other), zero cost, reported as exploratory; (iii) a second labeling of the six Reasoning-group cuts, about $25 at this run's rates (about $5 per early C-trace cut, about $2 for B10 and B11; Claude balance about $18), or about $3 if only the first 30 sentences of each continuation are labeled, which is all the first new node needs; either form is registered before it runs.
- A2, the order finding. P(C | ⟨ algebra --> famous problem ⟩) = 0.58 and P(E | ⟨ famous problem --> algebra ⟩) = 0.81 are pooled over all cuts, where the order is inherited from the prefix: 197 of the 225 ⟨ famous problem --> algebra ⟩ continuations come from E-trace cuts, and 236 of the 248 ⟨ algebra --> famous problem --> hedge word ⟩ continuations from C-trace cuts. Where the model chose the order itself, at cut-0, the split is 19 (16 of them E) against 4 (2 C, 0 E). As pooled, the numbers confound order with source trace. Two remedies: the order-swap intervention, which is what makes the finding stand (if it shows no effect, the third finding of the abstract is reworded before the deadline); and, free of cost, the same order analysis restricted to the three cuts whose prefix contains neither kind of sentence — cut-0, C-B00 and E-B00, 75 continuations — reported per cut, so that the prefix is held fixed inside each comparison (exploratory; to be computed from the judge labels when the results section is drafted; results v1 used cut-0 only).
- A3, the gap. The registered statistic Δ_(1) − Δ_(2) is the difference between the largest and the second-largest shift within each bootstrap draw and is non-negative by construction, so its interval ([0.00, 0.32] for the C-trace, [0.00, 0.28] for the E-trace) can touch zero and can never cross it; with n = 25 every Δ is a multiple of 0.04 and ties are frequent. "The interval includes zero" is therefore a boundary statement. The paper reports the registered statistic as registered and adds, marked exploratory, the signed difference between the two named blocks: Δ(C-B09) − Δ(C-B00) = +0.08, bootstrap 95% interval [−0.24, +0.40] (10,000 draws within the four cuts involved, seed 0; normal approximation from the four binomial variances, standard error 0.16, [−0.23, +0.39]); E-trace Δ(E-B00) − Δ(E-B03) = 0.00 [−0.32, +0.32]. Claude's computation of 2026-09-19 from continuations.jsonl; Kian re-derives it if it is used. The reading is unchanged — the two blocks cannot be ranked, and the near-tie rule still names both — but it no longer rests on a floored interval.
- A4, the p-values. With 10,000 permutations the smallest attainable p is 1/10,001, so "p = 0.0001" means that no permutation reached the observed S. The paper writes p ≤ 0.0001 with the number of permutations, and gives the E-trace result (p = 0.0033; four cuts in two groups) as the weaker one it is.
- A5, "three and a half times as far apart". S = 0.351 against a null mean of 0.100 is the C-trace (ratio 3.5); the E-trace ratio is 2.3 (0.090 against 0.040) and the pooled one 3.6 (0.329 against 0.091). The paper says which.
- A6, "a shift … is captured whole within one [block]". Stage one placed no cut inside a block, so the statement needs its evidence written out: each large rise is flanked by blocks that do not rise (C-B09 +0.56 between −0.08 and 0.00; C-B06 +0.40 between 0.00 and −0.20; C-B00 +0.48 followed by −0.28), so the rise is not spread over neighbours. What happens inside a block is known only from the archived sentence-level study (inside C-B09: old cut-59 0.60, s* +0.28, 0.88 after), at n = 25 and under the old cuts. The paper states the claim as a property of the construction supported by the flanking pattern, within the noise of n = 25; stage two would supply the within-block evidence and is not part of this paper unless Kian decides otherwise.
- A7, "Labels are human reviewed and reproduced by an LLM judge". Accurate as written, and the paper must say the rest: the labels of the two source traces were produced by an LLM assistant and reviewed by the author for face validity, not labeled blind by a human; the scheme's rules were written on the same two traces (pipeline decision 6); "reproduced" means Level 1 kappa 0.73–0.80 against the reviewed labels, block-boundary F1 0.91–0.93, first-node-after-cut agreement 0.90–0.93, test-retest kappa 0.90. The four-part judge statement (summary v6, Section 2d) and prompt v3 with its sha256 go into the appendix.
- A8, the level of the null (not a claim of the abstract; the first objection a reviewer will raise). The registered test is at Level 1, where one label covers unlike moves: in the C-trace's Reasoning group the last sentences before the cut are Reasoning > logical reasoning > algebra (C-B03, B04, B05), > calculation > algebra (C-B06), > hedge word analysis > everyday reading (C-B10) and > comparison > option vs option (C-B11), so a Markov chain over finer types could absorb part of the Level 1 spread. What survives the finest typing: C-B03, B04 and B05 end on the identical full path (the "Result:" sentence of each case of the enumeration) and their next-move distributions still differ — first new node Assumption in 13 of 24, 24 of 25 and 11 of 25 continuations — with S = 0.209 against a null mean of 0.078, p = 0.0006 (Claude's exploratory permutation test of 2026-09-19 on counts reconstructed from transition_pnext.csv, 10,000 permutations, seed 0; judge-dependent; Kian re-derives it if it is used). The paper reports the registered Level 1 test as registered, says what a finer chain could and could not absorb, and gives this same-path contrast as the exploratory answer: what differs between the three cuts is how many cases have already been tried, which is history, not type.
